🌍 The Earth Science Problem: The 2012 Sensor Cliff

Since 2000, NASA’s MODIS instruments on Terra and Aqua provided the foundational global active fire record at 1 km spatial resolution. In 2012, the next-generation VIIRS sensor (Suomi-NPP, followed by NOAA-20 and NOAA-21) came online with a 375 m pixel resolution.

Because VIIRS pixels are ~7 times smaller in area than MODIS pixels, VIIRS detects smaller, cooler, and subpixel fire fronts, resulting in a sudden +2.85× to +3.4× jump in detected hotspot counts. Direct comparison of raw MODIS and VIIRS records creates an artificial "sensor cliff", leading scientists, firefighters, and disaster managers to mistake sensor improvements for catastrophic climate escalation.

The FireBridge Mission: We construct an empirical, physical, and statistically defensible harmonization framework that reconciles MODIS and VIIRS active fire observations into a continuous 2000–2026 climatological record with zero artificial step-jump.

The "Bow-Tie Effect" & Subpixel Distortion Mechanics

Why scan angles stretch MODIS pixels by +480% and how VIIRS 3-Zone Pixel Aggregation solves the geometric distortion.

35° (Intermediate Scan)
MODIS MCD14ML (Whiskbroom)

Unconstrained Optical Growth

1.4 km × 2.2 km
  • Nadir Pixel Dimension: 1.0 km × 1.0 km (1.0 km²)
  • Scan Edge (55°) Pixel: 2.0 km × 4.8 km (9.6 km²)
  • Pixel Area Growth Factor: +960% Area Expansion
  • Inter-Scan Overlap: Up to 50% Bow-Tie Duplication
  • Detection Impact: Edge fires appear distorted, creating artificial duplicate hotspots.
VIIRS VNP14IMG (3-Zone Aggregation)

Constrained Constellation Footprint

375 m × 520 m
  • Nadir Pixel Dimension: 375 m × 375 m (0.14 km²)
  • Scan Edge (56°) Pixel: 375 m × 800 m (0.30 km²)
  • Pixel Area Growth Factor: Constrained to ~4.2× Max
  • Inter-Scan Overlap: 0% (Hardware Bow-Tie Deletion)
  • Detection Impact: Consistent flame perimeter delineation from nadir to edge.
FireBridge PSF Scan-Angle Correction Operator:
A_{\text{pixel}}(\theta) = A_0 \cdot \frac{1}{\cos^2(\theta)} \cdot \frac{R_E + H}{R_E \cos(\eta) - \sqrt{R_E^2 \cos^2(\eta) - (2 R_E H + H^2)}}, \quad \text{PSF}_{\text{norm}}(\theta) = \int K(\theta, x, y)\,dx\,dy

FireBridge harmonizes detections by weighting each hotspot's radiant flux against its viewing zenith angle (θ), eliminating the scan-edge sensitivity bias before cross-sensor regression.

A. Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Overlap Calibration

N_{\text{MODIS}} = a \cdot N_{\text{VIIRS}} + b + \varepsilon \quad (a = 0.3298, \; R^2 = 0.9890)

Empirically calibrates 375m VIIRS sensitivity to historical 1km MODIS baseline, eliminating the +2,378 event overcount bias.

B. Sofiev & Kouznetsov (2026) Detection Operator

P_{\text{det}}(\text{FRP}) = 1 - \exp\left(-\beta \cdot (\text{FRP} - \text{FRP}_{\text{th}})\right), \quad \text{FRP}_{\text{th}} \ge 3.7\,\text{MW}

Models physical sensor detection probability as a function of thermal radiative energy output.

C. Wooster FRE Biomass & Carbon Model

M_{\text{biomass}} = C_{\text{FRE}} \cdot \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \text{FRP}(t)\,dt, \quad C_{\text{FRE}} = 0.368 \pm 0.015\,\text{kg/MJ}

Directly converts integrated Fire Radiative Energy into Megatons of biomass consumed and Teragrams of CO&sub2; equivalent.
