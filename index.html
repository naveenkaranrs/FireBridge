⚠ Harmonization Scope & Operational Limitations

Validated Methods & Regions: Theil-Sen Robust Regression and Empirical Quantile Mapping (EQM) have been cross-validated across Northern California, the Punjab agricultural burning belt, and Southeastern Australia. While these methods remove the +280% to +380% sensor count step-jump, operational users should account for the following known physical and sampling constraints:

1. Diurnal Overpass Sampling Drift
MODIS Terra (10:30 AM local) and Aqua (1:30 PM local) capture different diurnal fire cycle phases than VIIRS (1:30 PM / 1:30 AM local). Midday fire escalation occurring between morning and afternoon overpasses is interpolated using climatological Gaussian curves.
2. View Zenith Angle (VZA) Pixel Growth
MODIS 1km pixels expand up to 4.8× at scan edges (55° VZA), increasing subpixel aggregation error. VIIRS 375m onboard 3:1 aggregation constrains swath-edge growth to 2×, introducing edge-of-scan geometry differences.
3. Cloud & Pyrocumulonimbus Obscuration
Passive 4µm/11µm thermal radiometers cannot penetrate thick optical clouds or dense pyrocumulonimbus smoke columns, causing temporary negative observation bias during explosive convective fire runs.
4. Biome-Specific Smoldering Regimes
Peatland and tropical smoldering fires emit low FRP invisible to MODIS but detected by VIIRS. Regional biomes require separate calibration intercepts; a single global multiplier cannot capture all combustion regimes.

ⓘ About FireBridge | NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026

FireBridge was developed for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge under the challenge track: "Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots".

It provides a continuous, open-science active fire climatology spanning from the launch of Terra MODIS (2000) to the latest operational VIIRS observations on NOAA-21 (2026), empowering emergency managers, firefighters, land managers, and carbon modelers with consistent long-term fire intelligence.
